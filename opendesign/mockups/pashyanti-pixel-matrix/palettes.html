Pashyanti / colour / six directions

Dot screen / 8 px lattice / v1

Palette directions

Read in daylight, one-handed, at 430 px: body at least 18 px, text at 7:1 measured rather than eyeballed, and every panel a flat hole punched in a lattice of round dots. These six families are the cloth-and-wall references that survive that floor; each is measured in the file and each can be flipped to its opposite ground.

Ground
An 8 px lattice of round dots; panels are flat holes.
Reader
Deaf and hard-of-hearing users; daylight, one hand, 430 px.
Floor
18 px body, 7:1 text, 44 px targets, visible focus.
Numbers
WCAG 2.x relative luminance, computed here, 2 decimals.

Each family is one light or dark ground with the same ten tokens and the same chat fragment, so the only variable is colour. 07 / recommendation

Indigo Resist for daylight, Peacock Matrix for the dark.

One pair, two grounds: the light family ships as :root and is the default on camera; the dark family is the reference the art direction came from and ships as .dark. Both are measured again in the drop-in block at the end.

01 Indigo Resist

ship as :root

Bone cloth under indigo is the daylight default: ink measures 13.77:1 and ink-2 9.25:1, so a 23 px Devanagari line stays legible after video compression, and the indigo dot lattice reads as woven cloth rather than noise.

Acid lime is held to one job, your reply leaving the phone, at 12.30:1 for the label riding on it; because the acid itself is only 1.21:1 against the bone field, every acid object carries a 1 px ink keyline, and that keyline is a rule, not a styling option.

04 Peacock Matrix

ship as .dark

The reference halftone, delivered without compromise: acid-lime dots at 8 px pitch on near-black, terracotta for outgoing audio and bone-white for reading, with ink/field at 16.19:1 and ink-2/field at 10.21:1, the highest of the dark grounds.

Keep it as the dark mode, not the daylight default: on a 430 px phone filmed in daylight the near-black field blooms and the lattice dissolves into noise, which is exactly the failure D-010 was written to avoid.

Twelve ground pairs were measured: six families, each flipped once. The lowest text ratio anywhere on the sheet is 8.20:1 (03 Chuna Lime-Wash, ink-2 on field), so no family hides a failure behind a pleasing swatch. The two lowest values among the non-text rows are the dot lattice on the two light flips (2.53 and 2.57), which is deliberate: the dots are texture and must never carry meaning.

08 / role legend

Ten tokens, ten jobs.

One warm colour for audio leaving, one acid for action, one cool for listening. Everything else is ink and holes; a token that is not doing one of those jobs is not in the palette.

  1. --field The page ground the lattice is printed on. The reference colour for every other ratio. App background, the transcript well, the cavity behind every hole. ground
  2. --panel A flat hole punched in the lattice: a surface where no dot moves under the words. Caller bubble, chips, status pill, composer, sheets, this specimen card. ink at 7:1
  3. --ink The other person's words, and every primary label that has to be read at a glance. Live transcript at 23 px, bubble text, headings, the send label. 7:1 on field and panel
  4. --ink-2 Secondary words: state, time, placeholders, captions. Never the transcript itself. Listening pill, timestamps, input placeholder, helper text. 7:1 on field and panel
  5. --line The 1 px rule: edges of holes, dividers, the keyline around an acid object. Bubble outline, chip outline, table rules, section edges. 3:1, non-text
  6. --signal The dot lattice itself. Texture, never meaning: it may be quiet, it may never be confusable with text. 8 px pitch ground behind the transcript and around every panel. 2.5:1, non-text
  7. --accent The one acid, reserved for your reply leaving the phone. Fill only; never a text colour. Send button, reply bubble, the checked state of the flip control. fill only
  8. --accent-ink The words that ride on acid. Never used on the field or a panel. Send label, reply text, the check mark inside an acid control. 7:1 on accent
  9. --hot One warm colour for audio in flight, outgoing: the voice is being spoken or played. Speaking dot, open beak, the live edge of the transcript. Never body text. 3:1, non-text
  10. --cool One cool colour for listening: the phone is open and the room is being heard. Listening pulse, live edge, focus ring; in the shipped pair it is also --accent-text. 3:1, non-text; 7:1 as text

Two hues carry state because state is geometry elsewhere (D-011, D-013): the ring pulses for listening, the beak opens for speaking. Nothing in this palette is a gradient, a shadow or a third accent, so a colour on screen always means one of these ten things.

  1. 01 Paint the lattice once, behind everything. body::before gets radial-gradient(var(--signal) 1.6px, transparent 1.7px) at 8px 8px, fixed, z-index: -1. Panels stay opaque, so a hole reads because the dots stop, not because of a shadow.
  2. 02 Square the shapes. .bubble, .btn-primary, .composer-input and .summary-card go to border-radius: 0; only the pill and chip keep 999px. --shadow-pop is now a hard 4 px offset: the language popover is the only thing that may use it.
  3. 03 Fix the two legacy pairings the remap exposes. .retry-badge must switch its text to var(--warn-ink), because --primary-ink is now dark-on-acid and measures 2.76:1 on --warn. .status-dot[data-state="speaking"] should paint var(--hot); listening already follows --accent-text to --cool.
  4. 04 Sakhi needs a keyline. The mascot fills with --color-accent, which is now acid: the trace measures 1.21:1 on the bone field, so give it a 1.5 px var(--ink) keyline or it disappears at 30 px.
