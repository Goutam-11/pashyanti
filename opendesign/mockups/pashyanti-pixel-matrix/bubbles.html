Pashyanti · dot screen · bubble treatments · 01

Ten ways to print a sentence.

Ten treatments for the same turn, drawn on one dot lattice so only the bubble changes. Caller lines are ink on a punched hole and carry hi · 412 ms; replies carry the warm signal and no metadata. A missed segment is deliberately absent: it is a notice, never a bubble, and must keep the loudest edge on the screen.

Samples are set at 21px on the 430px measure the app renders (a 366px bubble inside 16px gutters). The product floor is 18px and the current build sets 23px; the second control grows the samples to 24px to test that. Two stages are reprinted on paper stock.

State, not a second element

The reply block is already talking.

Audio in flight is a property of the block, not a new row. Three dots sit on the block's own bottom edge while the voice is being synthesised, and leave when it stops. No spinner, no status chip, no layout shift, no second bubble.

Comparison and recommendation

Ship one caller edge and one reply block.

Caller · ship01 Staircase slab
The caller's line is the reading task, so it should spend almost nothing on measure: 15.7:1 ink on panel, a 1px hairline (2px of measure), no vertical chrome, and the latency on a quiet 10px baseline inside the panel. Dissolve (02) is the more on-brand edge, but it spends the left edge — the scan anchor at 15 turns — on texture. Telegram (09) has the better metadata idea and charges 27px per caller turn, 405px across 15; Split register (07) charges 59px of width. Large text is the point, and the 430px screen has no width to give.
Reply · ship the structure06 Inverted signal block, corrected to warm
The block is right: a reply is short, loud and spoken, and one solid field reads at arm's length while the caller stays quiet. Acid is not: --accent on --accent-ink is 14.1:1, the best pair in the sheet, but it is the colour of a control, not of your own voice. Keep the chamfer, the waveform stamp and the no-metadata rule; set the bed to --hot-deep #8E3018 with --ink text (7.16:1). Raw --hot is a mark colour — 5.8:1 against the field, 5.5:1 against the panel, 2.9:1 as a bed for ink — so use it on edges and dots, never under or as text. The state strip tests that bed and its bottom row.
Reject03 Stamp
At 430px the bubble is 366px; a 10px ring each side takes 20px of measure and prints a 10px dot grid immediately beside 21px text — two textures at the same spatial frequency competing for the same eye movement, on the line the user must read fastest. The ring also spends the loud-edge vocabulary the "couldn't catch that" notice needs to stay unmistakable. On paper it holds contrast (14.8:1) but the perforation becomes a printed frame, so the hole-in-the-lattice idea dies. In a product whose rule is "large text is the point", spending 20px of the reading measure on ornament is the clearest disqualifier. It is the most charming treatment and the least readable one.

Computed from the tokens with the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula; the product rule is 7:1, so --hot is a mark colour, not a text bed unless it is deepened or lightened. The final row above is the important one for this direction: a panel is only 1.07:1 against the field, so the lattice, not the fill, is what makes a hole visible. Every edge in the sheet therefore either stops the dots, restarts them, or steps them — no border is doing the work that the texture should do.

What stays true across all ten

Caller text at 21px or larger, line height 1.42, no metadata under the reply, one warm signal per reply and none on the caller, and the notice for a missed segment left panel-free. Treatments 04 and 07 add a clock the transcript does not have; that field is a backend change, not a styling choice.

Bubble sheet 01 · dot-screen direction · tokens at :root · no build step, no local assets, fonts from Google Fonts only.

No server, no local assets, no fixed positioning: built to run from file:// and inside an iframe. Controls are 44px; the only motion is the speaking state, and it stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
