Pashyanti · background character sheet · pixel matrix

Six backgrounds that stay behind the words.

One 430 × 760 phone, one chrome, one variable: the field. The caller's speech is the loudest thing on this screen and Sakhi is its state indicator, so a background here may have character but not a voice. Every ratio below is read back off the canvas at load — no figure here is typed by hand, and the sample count is printed next to each one.

Contrast floor
Text ≥ 7:1 wherever it sits. The dark texture is capped at 1.5:1, which keeps ink-2 at 7.3:1 even without a panel; on paper a bare dot drops it to 5.9:1, so paper keeps every word on a panel. 04's five signal marks are the one exception.
Lattice
8 px base pitch, 6 px under the dock. Round dots, hard edges, no blur, no grain overlay. Panels are opaque holes with a 6 px punch-out margin.
Motion
Static unless the treatment is the motion study. Nothing loops when the app is idle, and prefers-reduced-motion removes the one drift entirely.

Reject for the product · 02 and 04

02 · Peacock watermark

A second peacock on the screen where the peacock is the state indicator. It cannot be quiet enough to stop explaining and loud enough to be worth its pixels, and the state read is the one thing this screen cannot afford to muddy.

The same objection rejects 04 as product motion: nothing should move when nothing is happening. Keep 02 for the share image and 04 for the video's title card — both are good pictures and neither is a good screen.

05 is not rejected. It is the summary screen's background, the one moment the page is a document and a hole in the lattice is the right register.

Read the six measured rows straight down: they report the same number. That is the finding, not a coincidence. An opaque hole means the field cannot reach a word, so all six clear the floor and the argument between them is character, not contrast. The one frame where the ground does touch type is E, below, and it is the only frame that had to buy a scrim.

How the numbers were taken

  1. Every ratio is computed at load. The canvas is read back with getImageData, each measured text node's ancestor backgrounds are alpha-composited over the sampled pixels, and the minimum WCAG ratio is reported.
  2. Worst text is the lowest ratio anywhere in the chrome — usually the 11 px meta line, not the 23 px bubble. Body copy is the two bubble texts alone, which is the number that actually matters to a reader.
  3. Art peak is the highest-contrast pixel outside any panel rectangle, measured against --field. It says how loud the texture is, not how safe the text is.
  4. 3 px stride for the background scan, 2 px stride inside each text box. Panels are measured from their live DOM rectangles, so a frame that is scaled down is measured at the scale it renders.

What was not verified

  • No screenshot in this environment. The peacock was checked numerically — winding numbers, bounding box, crop maths, an ASCII raster — but no one has looked at the rendered page in a browser.
  • Daylight legibility is asserted from the 7:1 floor, not from a lux meter or a phone held up to the sun.
  • Google Fonts is the only network dependency. Offline, the page falls back to system fonts and the Devanagari metrics change; the layout is in px so it holds, but the line breaks in the bubbles will differ.
  • Cost figures are dot counts and fill calls from the paint pass, not a profile on a low-end Android. The one motion is an 11 px CSS transform and was not measured on device.
